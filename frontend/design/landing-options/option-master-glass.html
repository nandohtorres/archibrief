How it works

From Terraform to a review you can check

The same steps run for every review, including the payments sample above.

  1. You submit Terraform and a brief

    A Terraform archive plus a few sentences about the workload: traffic, how available it must be, any data rules.

    The brief matters. "Customer-facing, can't go down" turns a single-AZ database from a footnote into a finding.

    payments-api.tarinput
    • main.tf
    • modules/
    • audit/s3.tf
    • api/lambda.tf
    • api/iam.tf
    • data/rds.tf

    Workload briefCustomer-facing payments API. Audit logs must be kept and never public. Moderate traffic, business-critical.

  2. Checks run, guidance is retrieved

    A trusted open-source scanner (Trivy) and ArchiBrief's own rules check the Terraform. Then the matching passages are pulled from AWS Well-Architected and security best-practice docs.

    Anything outside the submitted code is marked as a gap, never assumed.

    checksdeterministic
    • FAILLambda execution role grants s3:* on *
    • FAILAudit-log bucket has no public-access block
    • PASSRDS storage encryption is declared
    • GAPNo CloudTrail in the code; may exist at org level

    Retrieved: SEC03-BP02 · SEC08-BP02 · REL10-BP01

  3. You get a review with receipts

    A model weighs every result against your brief. Each citation is checked word for word against the AWS text before anything is published.

    Each finding: what it's about, the guidance it relies on, how sure it is, and what to change.

    report · findingoutput
    High risk

    Lambda execution role grants s3:* on *

    Evidence
    The inline policy contains Action s3:* and Resource *
    Cites
    SEC03-BP02 Grant least privilege access
    Recommendation
    Scope the role to the specific S3 actions the function uses, on the audit bucket only.

What it checks

  • Public exposureS3 public access, open security groups
  • Identity & accesswildcard IAM, unconditioned trust
  • Encryption at restS3, EBS, RDS storage
  • Recoverabilitybackups, versioning, point-in-time recovery
  • Availabilitysingle-AZ databases vs. your needs
  • Audit trailCloudTrail and log validation

Checked by Trivy's trusted rules plus ArchiBrief's own, then weighed against your brief.

What ArchiBrief won't claim

It never touches your AWS account. It reads only the files you submit. No credentials, no calls into your environment.

It can't see what you didn't submit. Org-level controls and unsubmitted modules show up as gaps, not passes or failures.

It's not a compliance audit. It's a cited second opinion to help you decide what to fix first.
